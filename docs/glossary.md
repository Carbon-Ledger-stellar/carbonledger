# Glossary of Carbon Market Terms

This glossary defines common terms used across carbon markets, with a focus on concepts relevant to this project. Terms are listed alphabetically. Where useful, entries link to related documentation.

> **Note:** Carbon market terminology varies between standards, registries, and jurisdictions. Definitions here are intended as a practical starting point for contributors, not as legal or regulatory guidance.

---

## A

**Additionality**
The requirement that a carbon credit represent emission reductions or removals that would not have occurred without the project's financial incentive. A project is "additional" if it goes beyond business-as-usual and is not required by law. Additionality is one of the most contested and important concepts in carbon markets. See also: *Baseline*.

**Avoided emissions**
Reductions that occur when an activity that would have generated emissions is prevented (e.g., protecting a forest from being cleared). Distinct from *removals*, which actively take carbon out of the atmosphere.

## B

**Baseline**
The reference scenario describing what emissions or removals would have occurred without the project. The baseline is used to calculate how many credits a project generates. Establishing a credible baseline is central to *additionality*.

**Buffer pool**
A reserve of credits set aside (often 10–20% of issued credits) to cover the risk of reversal, such as a forest fire destroying a reforestation project. Buffer credits are typically pooled across projects by a registry and cancelled if a reversal occurs.

## C

**Cap and trade**
A regulatory approach where a government sets a limit (cap) on total emissions and issues tradable allowances. Companies that emit less than their allowance can sell the surplus; those that emit more must buy allowances. See also: *Compliance market*.

**Carbon credit**
A tradable unit representing one *tonne of CO2e* that has been reduced, avoided, or removed from the atmosphere. Credits are issued by registries after *verification* and can be bought, sold, and *retired*.

**Carbon offset**
A credit used to compensate for emissions generated elsewhere. Buying an offset allows an entity to claim that its net emissions are lower. See also: *Carbon credit*.

**Co-benefit**
Additional positive outcomes of a carbon project beyond emissions reductions, such as biodiversity protection, job creation, improved air quality, or community development. Co-benefits are often highlighted in project documentation and can influence credit pricing.

**Compliance market**
A carbon market created and regulated by a government or intergovernmental body (e.g., the EU Emissions Trading System), where participation is mandated by law. Contrast with *Voluntary market*.

**Credit buyer**
An individual or organization that purchases carbon credits, either to meet regulatory obligations (compliance) or to offset voluntary emissions. Buyers should perform due diligence on *provenance* and *additionality*.

## D

**Double counting**
When the same emission reduction or removal is claimed or counted more than once — for example, by both the host country and the buyer's country, or by two different projects. Robust registries and *serial numbers* help prevent double counting.

## E

**Emissions trading**
A market-based mechanism where emissions allowances or credits are bought and sold. See *Cap and trade*.

## I

**Issuance**
The formal creation and registration of carbon credits by a registry after a project has been *verified*. Each issuance is assigned unique *serial numbers* to track the credits.

## L

**Leakage**
The situation where emission reductions in one area cause emissions to increase elsewhere (e.g., protecting one forest causes logging to shift to a neighboring forest). Credible methodologies require accounting for leakage.

## M

**Methodology**
A documented set of rules and procedures approved by a standard-setting body that defines how a project type calculates its *baseline*, demonstrates *additionality*, and quantifies emission reductions or removals. Projects must follow an approved methodology to be eligible for *issuance*.

**MRV (Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification)**
The process by which a project's emission reductions or removals are measured, documented, and independently checked. MRV underpins the integrity of every carbon credit.

## O

**Offset**
See *Carbon offset*.

## P

**Permanence**
The requirement that emission reductions or removals be maintained over the long term. Removals stored in forests or soils can be reversed (e.g., by fire or land-use change), so permanence risk is managed through *buffer pools* and long-term monitoring.

**Project developer**
The entity that designs, finances, and operates a carbon project. Developers are responsible for following an approved *methodology* and engaging with *MRV* and *verification*.

**Provenance**
The documented origin and history of a carbon credit — including the project, methodology, vintage, registry, and serial numbers. Buyers use provenance to assess quality and avoid *double counting*.

## R

**Registry**
A centralized system that records the *issuance*, transfer, and *retirement* of carbon credits. Registries assign unique *serial numbers* and provide transparency. Examples include Verra, Gold Standard, and government registries.

**Removal**
The process of actively taking carbon dioxide out of the atmosphere and storing it (e.g., through reforestation, soil carbon, or direct air capture). Contrast with *avoided emissions*.

**Retirement**
The permanent cancellation of a carbon credit so it can never be traded or used again. Retirement is how a buyer "uses" a credit to claim an offset. Retired credits are recorded in the *registry*.

## S

**Sequestration**
The capture and long-term storage of carbon dioxide, typically in forests, soils, oceans, or geological formations. See also: *Removal* and *Permanence*.

**Serial number**
A unique identifier assigned to each carbon credit by a *registry*. Serial numbers enable tracking of a credit's lifecycle and help prevent *double counting*.

**Standard (carbon standard)**
An organization that develops *methodologies*, accredits *verifiers*, and oversees *registries* to ensure the integrity of carbon credits. Examples include Verra, Gold Standard, and the Climate Action Reserve.

## T

**Tonne of CO2e (tCO2e)**
The standard unit of carbon credits: one metric tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent. Other greenhouse gases are converted to CO2e using global warming potentials so they can be compared and traded on a common basis.

## V

**Verification**
The independent assessment, by an accredited third-party *verifier*, that a project's reported emission reductions or removals meet the requirements of its *methodology*. Verification is a prerequisite for *issuance*.

**Verifier**
An accredited organization that performs *verification* of carbon projects. Verifiers must be independent of the *project developer*.

**Vintage**
The year (or period) in which the emission reductions or removals underlying a carbon credit occurred. A credit's vintage is a key attribute for buyers, as older vintages may be subject to different rules or perceived quality.

**Voluntary market**
A carbon market in which participants buy and sell credits without a regulatory obligation, typically to meet corporate sustainability goals. Contrast with *Compliance market*.
